About

I spent fifteen years working inside companies owned by self-made millionaires.

That's where the name comes from. Run It Like the Rich isn't a slogan I thought up — it's what I watched work, from the inside, in the back offices of people who had already done it.

I'm Laura Poincot. I've been in accounting since 1999 — twenty-seven years, and almost all of it in house, as the accountant and operations manager inside the business rather than a visitor to it.

For fifteen of those years I worked for a group of self-made multi-millionaires: a set of multi-entity partnerships with a lot of moving parts. Commercial real estate and development. The largest off-airport parking operation in Orlando. Auto salvage. Aviation — pilot services and aircraft rental. More than 115 commercial properties, where I dealt directly with the owners.

That last part is where the industry knowledge came from. Every one of those owners ran a different kind of business, under different county and state regulations, with different licenses, different insurance and a different idea of what "doing well" looked like. I sat with them. I learned what each operation actually takes to run, and where it breaks.

I have never been unable to fix a set of books.

Why I can fix the books other people give up on

Because I didn't only use the systems. I built them.

I've built general ledger systems, and I've built the ledger side for software that only had a front end — the operational part — and no accounting underneath it. That meant working with the coders and the people who invented the software, explaining which journal entries had to be written, and when, for the data to land in the general ledger correctly.

So I know the modules from the inside: how a sale in a point-of-sale system becomes a journal entry, how a payroll run posts, how a job-cost module feeds the income statement, and all the places those handoffs go wrong quietly. That knowledge is the whole key to cleaning something up. You can't repair a book you don't understand the plumbing of.

A strong database background takes it the rest of the way: financial system data repair, balancing and historical cleanup at the record level, not just the transaction level. Multi-year reconstructions. Balance sheets that have never tied. Equity and intercompany accounts nobody can explain. Conversions where the history came across wrong and everyone decided to live with it.

Even in the years I was in house, I always took cleanup projects on the side. That's what kept me sharp across industries instead of fluent in only one.

What the last five years have looked like

Almost continuous system and digital document resets, for owners getting their companies ready for valuation and sale.

That is a different standard from monthly bookkeeping. A buyer's team does not want a tidy P&L; they want to test it. They ask for three years of reconciled books, every contract, every license, and a list of who has access to what. Books that close each month are not the same as books that survive examination.

In the same period, AI and automation features rolled out in QuickBooks Online have damaged books that were previously clean — rules firing on the wrong transactions, categories rewritten in bulk, reconciliations quietly broken. Diagnosing that damage and undoing it has become a specialty of its own, and most owners have no idea it has happened until someone goes looking.

Why I built Run It Like the Rich

Working for those owners, I saw the difference up close. It was never flash. It was systems, discipline, and knowing their numbers were true. The ones who built real wealth had a back office they could see into; the ones who struggled were carrying it all in their heads.

Then I started seeing the same thing everywhere else: good businesses with good people, where the owner quietly isn't sure the books are right or the files are complete. Not because anyone was careless. Because every new tool and every new helper brought their own system.

That uncertainty is the real problem. It's what keeps an owner checking fifteen apps at night. And it's exactly what a buyer, a bank or a new manager will find first.

Run It Like the Rich is where I put the practical version of the fix: Admin, Books, Compliance, in that order.
