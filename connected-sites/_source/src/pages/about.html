About

Twenty-seven years inside other people's books.

I'm Laura Poincot. Since 1999 I have researched, found, converted, repaired and implemented the financial systems that owner-led companies run on — and spent the same twenty-seven years inside the numbers those systems hold.

Most accountants work at the transaction level. A strong database background takes my work past configuration and into the data itself: financial system data repair, balancing and historical cleanup at the record level.

That means the books that other people have given up on. Multi-year reconstructions. Balance sheets that have never tied. Equity and intercompany accounts nobody can explain. Conversions where the history came across wrong and everyone decided to live with it.

I have never been unable to fix a set of books.

What the last five years have looked like

Almost continuous system and digital document resets, for owners getting their companies ready for valuation and sale.

That is a different standard from monthly bookkeeping. A buyer's team does not want a tidy P&L; they want to test it. They ask for three years of reconciled books, every contract, every license, and a list of who has access to what. Books that close each month are not the same as books that survive examination.

In the same period, AI and automation features rolled out in QuickBooks Online have damaged books that were previously clean — rules firing on the wrong transactions, categories rewritten in bulk, reconciliations quietly broken. Diagnosing that damage and undoing it has become a specialty of its own, and most owners have no idea it has happened until someone goes looking.

Why I built Run It Like the Rich

I kept seeing the same thing: good businesses with good people, where the owner quietly isn't sure the books are right or the files are complete. Not because anyone was careless. Because every new tool and every new helper brought their own system.

That uncertainty is the real problem. It is what keeps an owner checking fifteen apps at night. And it is exactly what a buyer, a bank or a new manager will find first.

Run It Like the Rich is where I put the practical version of the fix: Admin, Books, Compliance, in that order.
